\begin{proposition}[what the kernel model's first pass produces]\label{prop:kernel-falls}
\Verified{$n=7,9$} Let the kernel model have, per S-box,
$B_i=a_ix_i^{2^{e_i}}+a_i^{2^{e_i}}x_i+a_i$ and $K_i=t_ia_i+a_i^{2^{e_i}}$. Then the degree-3
Macaulay pass adds exactly $6n=\#\mathrm{eqs}$ new quadratics, and they are the coordinates of
\[
  a_i\cdot K_i=t_ia_i^{2}+a_i^{2^{e_i}+1},\qquad
  t_i\cdot K_i=t_i^{2}a_i+t_ia_i^{2^{e_i}},\qquad i=0,\dots,\ell ,
\]
with nothing left over. These products fail to be cubic because $a_i^2$ and $t_i^2$ are
$\F_2$-linear and $\wt(2^{e_i}+1)=2$; no cancellation occurs. The $B_i$ contribute none.
\end{proposition}
\begin{remark}
This is why the control of \todo{E06 table} sees nothing: a random bilinear system of the same shape
has no Frobenius structure, so multiplying an equation by a variable does give a cubic. It is also
worth noting that the S-box relation $P_1^{(i)}$ is \emph{implied} by the model, through the identity
$B_i+x_iK_i=a_iP_1^{(i)}$, but is not itself in the degree-3 row space --- only the cubic multiple
$a_iP_1^{(i)}$ is.
\end{remark}
